Why mandates fail

Every failed AutoPay has a reason.
Most tools ignore it.

"Payment failed" is not a recovery strategy. The reason decides the move.

The problem is bigger than one bad month: NPCI's own figures show 4.82 crore NACH recurring debits returned against 19.36 crore presentations in August 2026 — roughly one in four recurring debits comes back (NPCI data, Aug 2026; includes re-presented debits and all return reasons).

Insufficient balance

The account was short at debit time. Right move: retry at the next payday window and nudge the customer with a one-tap payment link.

Paused or cancelled mandate

The customer paused or revoked the AutoPay mandate. Right move: stop hammering retries and ask them to re-authorise over WhatsApp.

Bank or rail downtime

The bank or UPI rails were down. Right move: retry later the same day, when the rails are back.

Limit exhausted

A daily or per-transaction limit blocked the debit. Right move: retry outside the busy window, or collect manually first.

Mandate expired

The mandate's validity ran out. Right move: re-enrol the customer before the subscription lapses.

Customer changed bank or card

The instrument behind the mandate is gone. Right move: a friendly re-authorisation link, not another declined retry.

FAQ

UPI AutoPay recovery, answered.

How common are failed recurring debits in India?+

Large and growing: NPCI's August 2026 data shows 4.82 crore NACH recurring debit returns against 19.36 crore presentations, about one in four recurring debits coming back. The count covers all return reasons and counts a re-presented debit each time it is presented, so it is not a clean first-attempt failure rate — but the scale is real, and it is why recovery, not prevention, is where the money is (source: NPCI recurring payments statistics, Aug 2026).

Why do UPI AutoPay payments fail?+

Most failures are routine: the customer's account is short of balance, they paused or deleted the mandate, the bank or UPI rails were down, a daily limit was hit, or the mandate expired. The reason matters because each one needs a different recovery move.

How does RecurSaathi recover a failed AutoPay payment?+

The failure reason is read from the gateway response, the retry is timed for that reason, and a WhatsApp and email nudge goes out with a one-tap payment link. If the customer replies with a promise to pay, the follow-up is rescheduled automatically.

Is retrying failed AutoPay debits RBI-compliant?+

RBI's recurring mandate rules, including the 24-hour pre-debit notification and the no-additional-authentication limit for smaller mandates, limit brute-force retries. RecurSaathi times retries inside the allowed windows and uses WhatsApp payment links for the rest.

Do I need Razorpay to use it?+

No. Manual UPI mode works from day one: generate a payment page and QR for your own UPI ID and mark the invoice paid when money lands. Connect Razorpay whenever you want automatic retries.

Do you hold my customers' money?+

No. Recovered payments land in your own gateway account or your own UPI ID. RecurSaathi automates, tracks and reports; money never flows through us.
